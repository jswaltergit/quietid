Attacks start with what's already public.

A home address, a personal phone number and a list of relatives are enough to make an attack convincing. Removing them takes away the starting point.

  1. Phishing and impersonation

    Personal details make fake messages and calls far more believable.

  2. Social engineering

    Attackers use family names, past addresses and employers to get past help desks and verification.

  3. Harassment and physical risk

    Public home addresses put people in visible roles, and their families, at risk.
